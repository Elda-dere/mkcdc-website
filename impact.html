Humanitarian Relief

From Conflict to Hope: Momina’s story

Erara, Shinile Zone, Somali Region · now in Eka Tafo, Sheger City

Illustrative image generated by AI.

Background

Momina Ebrahim Yuya, 35, is a mother of seven. She and her husband farmed over two hectares producing bananas, oranges, mangoes, guavas, sugarcane and other fruits. In 2017 a conflict destroyed their farmland and burned their assets, and the family fled. Resettled in Eka Tafo, they struggled with no income, limited food and medical costs for her husband and child.

What MKC-DC did

Her household received lifesaving support from MKC-DC, donated by Compassion International Ethiopia: 120 kg of rice, 12 kg of pulses, 4 liters of cooking oil and essential household items such as a mattress, blankets, carpets, buckets, jerrycans, cooking pots and serving plates.

Impact

  • Food security for two months
  • Dignity restored through basic household necessities
“Yesterday I was dependent, today I am able to help my community. Thanks to your support, my family is alive and hopeful again.”

From Conflict to Hope: Yasmin’s story

Jigjiga, Somali Region · now in Eka Tafo Sub City, Sheger City

Background

Yasmin is a 36-year-old mother of five, three of them boys. For many years she and her husband lived in Jigjiga, where he earned a living through electrical installations and pottery production. The 2017 conflict displaced them and they lost everything. In Eka Tafo her husband was diagnosed with asthma and could no longer work, the cost of treatment became unbearable, and two of her children were born with disabilities. She collected discarded plastic bottles to sell to recyclers, but the income was irregular and insufficient.

Challenges

  • No income
  • No food at home
  • Medical cost for her husband
  • Two children with disabilities

What MKC-DC did

Her household received lifesaving support from MKC-DC, donated by Compassion International Ethiopia: 120 kg of rice, 12 kg of pulses, 4 liters of cooking oil and essential household items such as a mattress, blankets, carpets, buckets, jerrycans, cooking pots and serving plates.

Impact

  • Food security for two months
  • Dignity restored through basic household necessities
“Yesterday I was dependent, today I am able to help my community. Thanks to your support, my family is alive and hopeful again.”
Livelihood and Food Security

From Subsistence Farming to Claiming a Share in a Central Market

Mr. Shabura, 42 · Bora district, Korke Gosse kebele

Background

Mr. Shabura is married with six children and farms two hectares that depend on rain. He lacked the knowledge and skills to diversify his income, and owned a traditional beehive but did not practice beekeeping management, so his honey was not enough even for his family.

What MKC-DC did

He was trained on the self-help group concept, benefits and formation, and organized into a self-help group. A loan from the group let him purchase modern beehives.

Impact

  • 34 beehives (30 modern and 4 traditional)
  • Harvests of 40 kg per modern beehive and 26 kg per traditional beehive
  • A total of 340,000 ETB (2,000 EURO) generated from honey production
“If people work hard on a small piece of land in full package, they can get a lot of production and hence diversify their income. It is as simple as that.”

Courage and Inspiration in the Middle of Hopelessness and Uncertainty

Mrs. Almaz Adugna, 48 · now in Wolkite town

Background

Mrs. Almaz has five children. She was a farmer in Bako Jelisa kebele, but was forced to leave her hometown and four hectares of land because of continuing conflict between government and armed groups. She had no prior knowledge on maximizing land productivity and now had to buy vegetables from the local market.

What MKC-DC did

She was trained on the self-help group concept, and on improved organic agriculture development: vermi compost preparation, conservation agriculture (CA) and sustainable organic agriculture (SOA).

Impact

  • Produced various vegetables and fed her family
  • Planning to produce coffee and avocado by applying the trainings received
“The land, hands, heart, and head were there. However, we were unable to feed ourselves. But now the veil is shattered, a possibility is at hand.”

An Inclusive Path to Resilience

Mr. Abera

Background

On 0.5 hectares, Mr. Abera struggled with declining cereal production caused by costly chemical fertilizers.

What MKC-DC did

After training in sustainable agriculture and crop diversification, and a 2,000 ETB (£9.13) loan from his self-help group, he expanded his livelihood by rearing egg-laying chickens alongside his regular farming.

Impact

  • Grows carrots, beetroot, beans, wheat and maize using organic methods
  • Produces enough food for his family
  • Earns an average of 15,000 ETB (£68.50) a year from selling surplus vegetables
“After receiving training in sustainable agriculture and crop diversification, my situation improved significantly.”
Child and Youth Development

Indale Tefera

Ambo

Background

Indale was born with a disability that left both of his legs paralyzed and backwards. He lives with his mother in Ambo. His father left, so she struggled alone to make ends meet. She heard about the Ambo MKC-CYD project and brought him to it.

What MKC-DC did

The project covered the cost of Indale’s medical treatment. He had surgery to have his leg amputated, and received a modern wheelchair and an artificial leg.

Impact

  • With his artificial legs Indale can now go to the toilet, the bathroom and school by himself

A Journey of Recovery: Living in the Aftermath

Alal Kebele, in the Gheralta District, has faced significant challenges. In addition to the consequences of the conflict in the Tigray Region, the community endured a devastating natural disaster in the summer of 2025. During the rainy season a severe storm brought heavy rain and hail, and the resulting floods swept away homes, belongings and crops.

Meserete Kristos Church Development Commission (MKC-DC) is a Christian organization dedicated to serving “The Whole Person” through community development programs. Thanks to the generous contributions of our donors, MKC-DC was able to provide much-needed cash assistance to the people. Below are some of the stories of change that have emerged from this support.
